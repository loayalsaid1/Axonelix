Left-Sided Heart Failure

Introduction to Left-Sided HF

Left-sided heart failure (LSHF) occurs when the left ventricle cannot pump blood efficiently to the rest of the body. This leads to blood backing up into the pulmonary circulation, causing pulmonary congestion and edema.

Figure 1.1: Schematic representation of left ventricular hypertrophy and chamber dilation.

1 Clinical Manifestations

Common symptoms result from pulmonary congestion and low cardiac output:

  • check_circle Dyspnea on exertion: The most common early symptom.
  • check_circle Orthopnea: Shortness of breath when lying flat, often measured by "pillow count".
  • check_circle Paroxysmal Nocturnal Dyspnea (PND): Sudden awakening at night with extreme dyspnea.
lightbulb Mnemonic: FORCED

Fatigue, Orthopnea, Rales, Cyanosis/Cough, Extremity weakness, Dyspnea.

High-Yield Insights

Systolic Failure

Reduced Ejection Fraction (HFrEF). Characterized by impaired contractility (e.g., post-MI).

Diastolic Failure

Preserved Ejection Fraction (HFpEF). Characterized by impaired relaxation/filling (e.g., HTN).
