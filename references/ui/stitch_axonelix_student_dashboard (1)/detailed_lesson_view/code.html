Acute Myocardial Infarction

Differentiation between STEMI and NSTEMI, pathophysiology, and immediate management protocols.

On this page Pathophysiology Clinical Presentation Diagnosis & ECG Management

Pathophysiology

Acute Myocardial Infarction (AMI) typically results from the rupture or erosion of a vulnerable atherosclerotic plaque. This event exposes the thrombogenic lipid core and subendothelial matrix to circulating blood, triggering platelet aggregation and thrombus formation.

zoom_in Click to expand

lightbulb Key Concept: Transmurality

STEMI involves full-thickness (transmural) necrosis of the myocardium due to total occlusion.
NSTEMI involves partial-thickness (subendocardial) necrosis due to partial occlusion or microembolization.

Diagnosis & ECG Findings

Feature STEMI NSTEMI / Unstable Angina
ECG Findings ST-segment elevation > 1mm in 2 contiguous leads. New LBBB. ST-segment depression, T-wave inversion, or normal.
Cardiac Biomarkers Elevated (Troponin I/T, CK-MB) NSTEMI: Elevated UA: Normal
Pathology Complete occlusion of coronary artery. Partial occlusion with preserved flow.

Clinical Pearl: "Time is Muscle"

Door-to-balloon time for PCI should be < 90 minutes. If PCI is not available within 120 minutes, fibrinolysis should be administered within 30 minutes of arrival (Door-to-needle).
