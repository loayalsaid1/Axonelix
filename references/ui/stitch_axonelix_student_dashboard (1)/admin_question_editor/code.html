Question Stem

A 65-year-old male presents to the emergency department with shortness of breath that has worsened over the past week. He reports sleeping in a recliner chair because he cannot breathe while lying flat. His past medical history is significant for hypertension and a myocardial infarction 5 years ago.

Physical examination reveals bilateral crackles at the lung bases and 2+ pitting edema in the lower extremities. Which of the following is the most likely underlying mechanism for his symptoms?

psychology Detailed Explanation

The correct answer is A. The patient's presentation of orthopnea (shortness of breath when lying flat), paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea, crackles, and peripheral edema is classic for congestive heart failure (CHF). In left-sided heart failure, the left ventricle fails to pump efficiently, causing blood to back up into the pulmonary circulation.

This backup leads to increased hydrostatic pressure within the pulmonary capillaries. According to Starling's forces, increased hydrostatic pressure pushes fluid out of the capillaries and into the lung interstitium and alveoli, resulting in pulmonary edema and the observed symptoms.

Why others are incorrect:

  • Option B: Decreased oncotic pressure is typically seen in hypoalbuminemia (liver failure, nephrotic syndrome), not primary heart failure.
  • Option C: Increased permeability is the mechanism in ARDS or pneumonia (inflammatory causes).
